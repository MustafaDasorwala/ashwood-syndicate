The Human Condition Architecture (Beyond Basic Psychology)

To create a fully realized human being, we must layer deeply ingrained beliefs, social bonds, and past experiences over their baseline personality traits. A highly cowardly person (High Neuroticism) will still fight to the death if someone threatens their child (Strong Attachment). This architecture dictates the exceptions to their personality rules.

Layer 1: Moral Foundations Theory (The Compass)

Developed by social psychologist Jonathan Haidt, this theory explains why people with similar personalities have vastly different beliefs. It dictates what a character views as "sacred" versus "profane." We assign a weight (1-10) to each foundation.

Care / Harm: How much do they value protecting the vulnerable? (High Care means they will reject a lucrative bribe if it hurts a child).

Fairness / Cheating: Do they believe in justice and proportionality? (High Fairness means they hate thieves, even if the thief is their ally).

Loyalty / Betrayal: Do they prioritize their "tribe" (family, faction, town) over outsiders? (High Loyalty means they will lie to protect a guilty friend).

Authority / Subversion: Do they respect tradition, law, and hierarchy? (High Authority means they will obey the Sheriff, even if the Sheriff is cruel).

Sanctity / Degradation: Do they believe in purity (religious, bodily, or ideological)? (High Sanctity means they might refuse to work with a known drunk or criminal, regardless of the logic).

Gameplay Impact: When the player proposes an action, the AI evaluates it against their Moral Foundations first. If it violates their highest moral score, they will reject it vehemently, even if their Maslow's "Need" is desperate.

Layer 2: The Web of Attachments (Sociology)

Humans do not exist in a vacuum. Their behavior is heavily modified by who they care about. Every NPC must have a web of deeply rooted connections that override their standard logic.

Kinship / Blood Ties: Unbreakable bonds. An NPC will sabotage their own goals to save their sibling.

The Life Debt: A profound sense of obligation based on a past event.

The Arch-Rivalry: Deep-seated, irrational hatred. If an NPC has a rivalry with the Blacksmith, they will vote against the player simply because the Blacksmith voted for the player.

Romantic Entanglement: Secret lovers, unrequited love, or bitter exes. These create massive blind spots in a character's logic.

Gameplay Impact: The player can use these attachments as leverage. You don't need to bribe the Mayor if you can blackmail the Mayor's son.

Layer 3: Cognitive Biases & Blind Spots

Humans are irrational. We don't process data perfectly. Giving NPCs specific cognitive biases makes them feel authentic and flawed.

Confirmation Bias: If an NPC already hates the player, they will easily believe rumors that the player is evil, but will require massive proof to believe the player did something good.

Sunk Cost Fallacy: If an NPC has already invested a lot of time or money into a failing faction, they will double down rather than admit they were wrong.

The Halo Effect: If an NPC finds a character highly attractive or charming, they will ignore red flags about their Dark Triad traits.

Layer 4: Cognitive History (The Core Memory / Trauma)

Every well-written character has a "ghost" or a formative event that permanently warped their worldview.

The Trigger: A specific subject or action that bypasses all logic and induces an immediate extreme emotional response (Panic, Rage, Shutdown).

Example: An NPC survived a terrible fire. If the player threatens them with arson (even as a bluff), their AI logic bypasses "Evaluate Intimidation" and goes straight to "Flee Town."

The Aspiration (The Dream): Distinct from an immediate Maslow's Need. A character might need food today, but their 10-year dream is to open a theater.

The Synthesis: The Complete Human Object

When generating an NPC for a playthrough, the engine combines all previous documents into one complete human profile:

{
  "Identity": "Elias the Miller",
  "Personality_OCEAN": {"O": Low, "C": High, "E": Mid, "A": Mid, "N": Low},
  "Dark_Triad": {"Machiavellianism": Low, "Narcissism": Low, "Psychopathy": Low},
  "Current_Need": "Safety (Owing money to the Syndicate)",
  "Morality": {"Highest": "Authority", "Lowest": "Care"},
  "Attachments": {"Loyal_To": "The Church", "Hates": "Outsiders"},
  "Bias": "Confirmation Bias against strangers",
  "Core_Trauma": "Betrayed by a smooth-talking merchant in his youth (Trigger: Flattery)"
}


Result: A rigid, rule-following man who respects the law above human compassion. He is currently in danger, but because of his past trauma, if the player tries to use Flattery to win him over, he will instantly become hostile. He must be approached with hard facts and appeals to Authority.
